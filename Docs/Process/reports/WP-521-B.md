## Change requests filed (FIX-4)

**Change request A — the reading of RT-2 for frame-wise nodes.**

- What: RT-2 (§5.6) says "Dynamic dispatch is allowed once per kernel or node per block, never inside a per-sample loop." It does not say whether a node that works frame by frame may dispatch once per frame, or a node that cuts a block into chunks once per chunk. This batch has three such calls: `ConstantQNode.transform` calls `plan.forward` through `any FFTPlan` once per completed octave frame (`Nodes/ConstantQNodes.swift:393`; at the defaults, hop 512 and blocks of 4 096, up to 56 calls a block over the 7 octaves); `HalfBandDecimator.process` calls the C decimator through its function pointer once per octave per chunk of at most 1 024 samples (`:145`); `ChromaFoldNode.process` calls `normalization.norm(of:)` through `any ChromaNormalization` once per frame (`Nodes/ChromaNodes.swift:59`). The STFT node of M4, verified and audited, does the same (`Nodes/STFTNode.swift:88`).
- Why: read word for word, each of these breaks RT-2; read by the rule's purpose (no dispatch inside a per-sample loop, a bounded cost per block), none does. Two verifiers can grade the same line differently, which the audits of the first wave asked to avoid.
- Evidence: the lines above; every allocation audit of these nodes passes and fails when an allocation is added (WP-521-B.md, WP-524-B.md); the counting backend shows one transform per octave per hop (`r13_oneTransformPerOctavePerHop`).
- Proposed wording (§5.6, RT-2, last sentence): "Dynamic dispatch — through a protocol, an existential, a closure or a C function pointer — is allowed once per kernel or node per block, and, for a node that works frame by frame or in chunks, once per frame or chunk for each member it holds (a transform plan, a normalisation, a C kernel); never inside a per-sample or per-bin loop."
- Sections affected: §5.6 RT-2; nothing else (§11.5 lens R refers to RT-1…RT-9).

**Change request B — NUM-1 and finite input at the edge of `Float`.**

- What: NUM-1 (§6.1) says "No NaN, no infinity", that every division states its result for a zero or sub-floor denominator and every logarithm has a floor, and that a non-finite sample arriving from a source is replaced by 0 at the source adapter. It says nothing about finite input so large that the arithmetic of a correct algorithm overflows.
- Why: in this batch finite input gives infinities or NaNs at four places, each a blocking finding under the present wording: the transform of samples at ±greatestFiniteMagnitude (B-7), the pseudo constant-Q of magnitudes at greatestFiniteMagnitude (B-10, WP-523), the L2 chroma norm of finite values from 2·10¹⁹ up and the L1 norm at greatestFiniteMagnitude, with the chroma of such frames read as zeros (B-13, WP-524), and `ConstantQGeometry.frequency(ofBin: .max)`, which returns +∞ (B-2, WP-517). The same holds for every sum, square or transform of the package (an FFT, a mean square) at full-scale `Float`: each step must then guard its result or scale its input, at a cost per frame. The owner should say which is meant.
- Evidence: the probes named in B-2, B-7, B-10 and B-13 (`VerifierB/Probes/VerifierBProbeTests.swift`, `VerifierB/logs/probes-*.txt`).
- Proposed wording, one of two (§6.1, NUM-1): either (1) keep the rule absolute and add: "A step whose arithmetic can overflow for finite input — a sum, a square, a transform — scales so that it cannot, or replaces a non-finite result by zeros and counts it; this holds for samples up to ±`Float.greatestFiniteMagnitude`"; or (2) bound the input: "A finite sample of magnitude above 2²⁰ (about 120 dB above full scale) is clamped to ±2²⁰ at the source adapter and counted, as a non-finite one is replaced; within that bound no step produces NaN or ∞, and a step's tests show it at the bound."
- Sections affected: §6.1 NUM-1; §4.5 (the source adapters) under option 2; the probes of audit demand 6 would then use the bound in place of `greatestFiniteMagnitude` for samples. Until the owner decides, B-2, B-7, B-10 and B-13 stay blocking under the present text.
