## What

1. **`.perChannel` on a signal of several channels is refused** at attach by `STFT`, `Level` and `Waveform`, with an error that says to write one `Tap` per channel, each with `channels: .channel(n)`. §5.3 lists `.perChannel` and says nothing of what follows a framing step that keeps the channels apart: a spectral scope carries one sequence of frames, and a feed has one writer. On one channel the three policies are the same. The owner decides what `.perChannel` delivers.
2. **References (STG-2).** librosa is not installed, so parity with `librosa.stft` and `librosa.feature.mfcc` is replaced by references written with NumPy and SciPy from the published definitions; each generator says so. No recordings were supplied, so the speech and music fixtures are synthetic (1 s and 2 s at 22 050 Hz); the music fixture carries a noise floor at −46 dB, without which bins under the floor of a single-precision transform differ by tens of decibels between the package and a double-precision reference.
3. **The delay of `STFT` then `InverseSTFT`** is frame − hop samples (or half a frame when centred, if that is more), not "the frame" as REAL-5 puts it.
4. **`InverseSTFT` repeats the window, the normalisation, the alignment and the backend** of its `STFT`: the scope that travels with the signal carries the frame, the hop and the axis only. `STFT.inverse` is the matching value.
5. **Defaults**: alignment `.causal`; `Spectrogram` normalises by amplitude, `MelSpectrogram` and `MFCC` not at all; `CepstralPitch` looks between 50 and 500 Hz and refuses a frame that does not reach the longest period, naming the transform length that would.
6. **A complex cepstrum frame** holds M + 2 values: the M quefrencies, then the delay removed (over M) and the sign of bin 0, which the inverse needs.
7. **`Decibels` is a step family, so it is not an `InvertibleStage`**: it has `inverse(of:)` and `inversionReport(for:sampleRate:after:)` of its own.
8. **`Waveform(resolution:)`** takes seconds; a frame's time is that of its first stretch.
9. **`Window.analysis(frame:hop:tail:)`** takes the window's function again for the questions about the whole line: a `Window` keeps samples of its closure, not the closure.
10. **`InversionReport.predictedError` is a sum that is not capped**: above 1 it says that nothing is predicted, and it is infinite for silence at a floor, where the measured relative error is infinite too.
11. **Register row 1 (§7.5)**: the floor of the single-precision transform measured here is 0.5 to 1.7·10⁻⁷ of the frame maximum (16 to 16 384 points), up to twice the 8·10⁻⁸ of E-30. **Row 22**: the measured error of an STFT round trip grows as √(B/A) of the frame bounds, not as B/A; the predicted bound keeps B/A.
12. **ORD-4 for a block that begins with a step of a family**: the catch-all cannot know what the block was meant to follow, so each family names the spectrum it most often follows. A branch that taps another spectrum than a step takes is not enumerated family by family.
13. **WP-511 is met narrowly** at 110 Hz: 0.97 % against 1 % (frame 4 096, 20 partials).
14. **`ContextDependentStep`** (the protocol of a step whose output depends on what it follows) is public in `ZTronDSPAnalysis`, not in the core: a family written outside the package uses the same shape, and may want it one target down.
15. **Additions to the public surface** the specification does not name: `ContextDependentStep`, `FFTPlan`, `VDSPFFTBackend`, `FrameAlignment`, `WindowNormalization`, `FrequencyRange`, `Framer`, `LinearValueKind`, `MelNormalization` and its two values, `MelFilterBank`, `LevelKind`, `MeasuredSpectrum`, `CepstrumKind`, `InversionCondition`, `InversionRoundTrip`, `InversionMeasurement`, `InversionContext`, `ScalarTrack`, `DSP.realDFT` and `DSP.inverseRealDFT`.

## Why

Item 1 is a gap of §5.3. Item 2 is what the host allows. Items 3 and 11 correct figures of the specification by measurement. The rest are choices the specification leaves to the implementation.

## Evidence

`ZTronDSPAnalysisTests`: 200 tests in 17 suites, Debug and Release. Golden vectors from the pinned NumPy and SciPy (`fixtures`, `spectral_transforms`, `mel`, `cepstrum`, `window_analysis`, `matlab_scripts`). The order matrix (`Docs/OrderMatrix.md`, `OrderMatrixTests`): the compiler and the rules agree on every stage of the target after every domain. Stability notes: `Docs/Numerics/real-fft.md`, `spectral-centroid.md`, `mel-filter-bank.md`, `mel-projection.md`, `cepstrum.md`, `complex-cepstrum.md`, `window-gains.md`, `window-smoothness.md`, `window-spectrum.md`, `inversion-analysis.md`.
