## What

1. **A plan on its way in is replaced only by an update that is accepted.** An update that arrives while a plan is pre-rolled, or before the analysis side has taken it, replaces that plan once the new pipeline is compiled and every sink of it is claimed and prepared. An update that is refused replaces nothing: the plan on its way in takes over as if the refused update had not been made. An update that arrives while a plan is being faded in lets that plan take over at once, as before (CR-015 item 5), also when the update is then refused.
2. **A sink of a plan that is replaced goes over to the plan that replaces it** when that plan writes it too: it is prepared for the new frames and is not released in between (ATT-5). A sink that only the replaced plan wrote is released at once.
3. **Commands reach the plan they were sent to before another plan takes its place** (SWAP-4). At a block boundary, and when the control context installs a plan itself, queued values and switch positions are applied first; what was sent to a plan that is replaced before it ran is applied to it before it goes.
4. **A stage that is an identity by its values keeps its place in the topology** (§5.5.4, F1, F4). A gain at 0 dB, gains per channel all at 0 dB and a single unit tap have the topology of the same stage with other values; adjacent gains fuse whatever their values. Sent to 0 dB, a running gain is sent the value: its kernel stays and ramps, and a factor at rest on 1 is not applied. A plan compiled while the stage is an identity runs no kernel for it (F4); when its values then change, a plan is compiled whose main line is the running one with the stage's kernel added as that identity, it takes over at the next block boundary without pre-roll and without a fade, the values are sent to the kernel by command and ramp over 10 ms, and the session reports `.replanned(.update)`. In both directions the stages, branches, sinks and feeds behind the gain are the ones that ran (SWAP-1).
5. **A switched group runs as one kernel only when its kernels keep the rate and none has a bound switch of its own** (ACT-6). Any other group of kernels runs its stages one by one between the group's switch points: a bound switch inside switches its stage, a rate change inside loses no sample, and the bypass delays by the group's latency in seconds (ACT-8, REAL-5).
6. **On the main line a kernel or node is moved into a new plan only when every kernel and node in front of it is moved too** (SWAP-1). What stands behind the first stage that differs is made anew, pre-rolled and cross-faded with it (SWAP-2). Before, such an object was run by both plans during the transition, twice per block.
7. **Frames dropped upstream are reported behind a processed source** (RT-8, SRC-2): as `.overrun` on the processed source's `events` and to the sessions attached to it, counted in frames of the processed signal (rounded up, at least one), down a chain of processed sources.
8. **A plan no machine is asked to hold is refused before anything is allocated** (§9.3): one buffer holds at most 2²⁸ samples (frames times channels), a plan at most 2²⁴ kernels or channels; more is `.resourceAllocationFailed`.
9. **An exchange is reported with what the analysis side said of it, whoever books it** (SWAP-2): booked by the next update or by a stop, it still reports `.outputChanged`.
10. **A figure a caller states is counted before anything is made for it** (§9.3, REAL-5). A ramp, a latency, a settling time, a block size or a rate is counted in frames where the pipeline is compiled or the source attached. A figure that is no count of frames, or more than the place counts (2³² − 1 frames for a kernel or a ring, 2²⁸ samples for a buffer of a plan), is refused: `.invalidParameter` with the stage's path and `ramp`, `latency`, `latencyFrames`, `settlingFrames` or `sampleRate`, or `.resourceAllocationFailed` ("the buffers of a group" with the group's path, "a ring of C channels of N frames"). Nothing that is accounted for is cut to fit; a ramp or a latency below zero is none.
11. **A stage leaves a positive, finite rate and a finite latency behind it**, or is refused where the pipeline is lowered (§5.11.1).
12. **Fusion and figures** (F1, F3): two gains fuse only when both ramps are counts of frames at their rate; two FIR filters fuse only when their latencies, each at least zero, add up to at most 2³² − 1 frames, and the fused filter states that sum.
13. **A switched group that runs as one kernel is refused** when its kernels' latencies add up to more than a buffer of a plan holds (ACT-8).
14. **`RingWriter`** takes frame counts as the ring counts them: a count below zero is none, and one above 2³² − 1 is that many (the ring writes what fits and counts the rest as dropped).
15. **Non-finite input behind a processed source** (NUM-1): a session attached to a processed source is told of non-finite input replaced at the source adapter in front, with the count of samples replaced there.
16. **The thread that runs a plan reads nothing of the plan's description** (RT-4): the fade length and the pre-roll of an exchange are constants of the plan, and everything the analysis side reads of a plan is immutable.
17. **Booking an exchange** (SWAP-2): the runner says what it has to say of an exchange before it shows the plan's generation, and the control context books an exchange when it sees the generation, from its service, the next update or a stop alike.
18. **A switch runs a stage unheard for its latency in full and its settling time capped at one second** (ACT-7, SWAP-2; added by the fourth part of the fix cycle, round 3). ACT-7 runs a stage "for its latency plus its settling time", SWAP-2 defines a settling time as "declared by each stage, capped at one second": the switch of a kernel in the C layer and a group switched in line now agree on that reading. SWAP-2's own pre-roll of an exchange keeps its cap of one second in all.
19. **The C layer adds frames in 64 bits** (§9.3, REAL-5): a switched kernel's latency and settling time, the pre-roll position and a plan's latency saturate at 2³² − 1 frames; a plan run as one kernel whose kernels' latencies exceed 32 bits gives a null kernel and stays the caller's, and its settling time saturates.
20. **A pipeline's result rate reaches the binding pump** (OUT-2, OD-15): it is the highest rate among the environments of the bound results; a rate that is not positive and finite is refused where it is set (`.invalidParameter` with the group's path and `resultRate`). A changed result rate compiles a new plan.
21. **What a stage's first `process` asks of the Swift runtime is reached on the control context** (RT-2). The stock sources reach it when their event channel is made, once per process. A plan is not primed on the control context: stages taken over from the running plan run on the analysis thread at that moment, `process` and `reset` are calls of the analysis thread, and one more of each is visible to every stage that counts them. A stage written outside reaches it in its `make`, and holds it with an audit that opens before the first block (`Docs/ExtensionGuide.md`).

## Why

Items 1 to 3, 6 and 9 settle cases the SWAP rules and §4.5 leave open, each after a defect was found there. Item 4 reconciles §5.5.4 ("gain amounts are values") with fusion F4, which removes an identity: without it a gain that an animation lands on 0 dB changed the topology (ANI-2…ANI-5). Item 5 is what ACT-5, ACT-8 and REAL-5 require of a group that the earlier implementation ran as one nested plan. Items 7, 8 and 10 to 15 fill gaps of RT-8, NUM-1 and §9.3; items 16 and 17 close two unsynchronised reads between the analysis thread and the control context.

## Evidence

`Docs/Process/reports/fix-cycle-1-WP-315-342.md` (parts 2 to 4; items 18 to 21 in part 4) and `fix-cycle-1-WP-201-227.md` (second part): the defects F1 to F8 with their tests (`UpdateRegressionTests`, `SwitchedGroupRegressionTests`, `UnityGainRegressionTests`, `PlanSizeRegressionTests`, `ProcessedSourceTests`, `GainCascadeTests`), most of them seen failing with the fix undone. Every test target passes in Debug and Release on the tree that holds these changes, and the three targets of the core, the C layer and the test product pass under the Thread Sanitizer with no report.

## Not done, and why

- An update that is refused while a plan is being faded in still cuts the fade short (item 1 says so; nothing in the specification asks for more).
- The 32-bit sums of the C layer's switch, the cap of its pre-roll, and the first render of a `ScriptedSource` that allocated in an optimised build: done in the fourth part (items 18, 19 and 21).
