## What

The `ALG` packages made their currency checks offline, against the register alone. The search that §7.3 asks for was made afterwards, row by row, and is recorded in `Docs/Numerics/currency-2026-11.md`. Every selected algorithm is kept. Seven things are put to the owner:

1. **Row 6, Savitzky–Golay.** Schmid, Rath & Diebold (2022) show that the Savitzky–Golay kernel passes strong components above its cut-off, which costs noise suppression, above all in derivatives, and that it misbehaves at the edges of the data; they propose a convolution kernel built on a windowed sinc with corrections ("modified sinc"), and Savitzky–Golay with fitting weights. The package keeps `SavitzkyGolay`, which G-3 asks for by name. Proposed: register the modified-sinc smoother as the alternative of row 6, and decide whether a `Smooth(.modifiedSinc(…))` stage is wanted beside it.
2. **Row 16, median filtering.** Adams (2021) gives sorting networks for two-dimensional medians of any size that are more than ten times faster than earlier work. They are patented (US 11,756,175). The package filters along one axis at a time, which that work does not concern. Proposed: no entry in the register; the note stays here so that a two-dimensional median, should one be wanted, is not built on it unawares.
3. **Row 13, constant-Q.** The sliding constant-Q transform (Bradford, ffitch & Dobson 2008) updates every bin at every sample: the lowest latency there is, at a cost per sample. Proposed: register it as a second alternative beside the non-stationary Gabor transform.
4. **Row 3, IIR filtering.** A recursive filter can be unstable while its coefficients move between two stable settings (Laroche 2007), and direct forms are the weak case; trapezoidal state-variable structures are the usual answer for fast modulation. The package changes single-precision coefficients through vDSP's own ramp (E-08) and exchanges double-precision ones at a block boundary, and CLICK-1 is tested for a step of a tenth of an octave. An animated cutoff (ANI-2) sends such steps at the display rate. Proposed: register the state-variable structure as an alternative of row 3, to be taken up if an animated filter is found to click beyond CLICK-1.
5. **Row 14, onset envelope.** SuperFlux is still the reference; a 2024 preprint reports parity with it at lower cost on instruments with a fast attack. Proposed: nothing now; to be looked at again at the next release.
6. **Row 20, band-boost.** Vicanek published a matched second-order Butterworth shelving design in 2024, the counterpart for shelves of the matched peak the register already names. Proposed: register it as the alternative for `LowShelf` and `HighShelf`.
7. **Row 19, cepstrum.** The complex cepstrum needs the phase unwrapped, which the usual adaptive methods can get wrong on hard frames; the cepstrum can also be computed from the roots of the frame's polynomial, with no unwrapping at all, exactly and at a far higher cost. Proposed: register that as the alternative of row 19 for the complex cepstrum offline; the inversion report already flags the frames on which unwrapping is in doubt.
8. **Row 4, Bessel polynomial roots** (added 2026-11-18 by the supplement of the currency record, rows 3a to 4c). Dunster, Gil, Ruiz-Antolín & Segura give an algorithm for the zeros of the reverse generalized Bessel polynomials (2021) and uniform asymptotic expansions for all of them, accurate for moderate and large degree (ETNA 65, 2026). The package takes Campos's starting values and refines them by Aberth's iteration on coefficients carried in double-double, which meets its row with room to spare up to order 25. Proposed: register the newer method as the alternative for Bessel prototypes of high order; nothing is adopted now.
