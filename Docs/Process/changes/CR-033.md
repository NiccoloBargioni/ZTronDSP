## What

VIS-2 reads: "Every public symbol is documented with its threading contract: which context may call
it, and whether it may allocate." It is proposed to read:

> **VIS-2** Every public symbol is documented with its threading contract: which context may call
> it, and whether it may allocate. A type's statement of context and allocation covers its members;
> a member states its own contract only where it differs from its type's (a method callable from
> the analysis thread only, a `process` that must not allocate). A member that implements a protocol
> requirement has the requirement's contract, which the protocol documents, unless it states
> another. A declaration marked `@available(*, unavailable)` that exists only to give a diagnostic
> (ORD-4) carries a documentation comment that says so, and no contract. A nested type, and a
> member that a module adds to a type of another module, state their own contract.

The vocabulary a contract is written in is that of §4.3, by the names it gives the contexts (control
context, analysis thread, I/O thread, main thread or main actor, the background executor, any
thread), with one of "allocates", "may allocate", "does not allocate", "never allocates".
`Scripts/lib/doc_audit.py` documents the vocabulary it recognises at its top: "control or
background context" names the control context, a plural subject takes "do not allocate", and an
adjective ("allocation-free") is not recognised, so the package writes "does not allocate".

## Why

1. **Applied as written, VIS-2 asks for a contract on every member**, including the ones whose
   contract is already said where it belongs. Verifier A of WP-515 counted 57 public declarations
   of one batch without a `///`; most were witnesses (`stageDescription`, `lower(_:)`,
   `typealias Input`, `makeTransform`, `mask`, `validate`), whose contract is the requirement's.
   The verifiers of waves 1–3 graded each omission blocking (about fifty findings), and refused a
   member with no `///` of its own unless its type said that its statement covers its members.
2. **For an overload that cannot be called, a threading contract has no meaning.** The
   `buildPartialBlock` overloads of `Order/OrderDiagnostics*.swift` and the unavailable fluent
   methods exist to carry an ORD-4 message; no context ever runs them.
3. **A member added to another module's type** (`CrossoverTable.replacing(_:with:)` of
   `ZTronDSPMetal`, the `Pipeline` methods of `ZTronDSPDesign` and `ZTronDSPAnalysis`) has no type
   of its own module whose statement could cover it, and a nested type is a type: both state their
   own.

## Evidence

- `Docs/Process/reports/WP-515-A.md`, finding A-6 and CR-VA-4 (the proposed wording this request
  takes up and completes with the last sentence).
- WP-901b's audit, `Scripts/docs.sh --audit`, implements exactly this reading: check `[no comment]`
  asks every public symbol for a comment of its own (the symbol graphs are extracted with
  `-skip-inherited-docs`, so a comment a witness only inherits does not count); check
  `[no contract]` asks the contract of every public type, protocol, global function and member of
  another module's type, a witness of a requirement whose comment, or whose protocol's, states both
  halves being exempt; check `[source]` asks an unavailable declaration for a comment that names
  ORD-4, "unavailable" or "diagnostic". Its self-test (`Scripts/tests/test-docs.sh`) holds each of
  these cases.
- Because the verifiers applied VIS-2 as written until the owner decides, the package also writes a
  short `///` on every public member: on 2026-11-21 the audit finds, among the 2 991 public symbols
  of the seven Swift modules that declare any, none without a comment of its own (12 of them are
  written by the compiler, `init(rawValue:)` and `init(from:)`, and have no declaration to comment)
  and none without a contract where one is asked; the source scan finds none of the 2 676 public
  declarations without a `///` directly above (`Docs/Process/reports/impl-WP-901b.md`).
