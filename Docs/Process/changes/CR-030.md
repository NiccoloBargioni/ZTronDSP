## What

§6.3.4, row "the same families, digital low-pass, and the upper skirt of a digital band-pass", says the slope is "within ±0.5 dB per octave in octaves that end at or below f_s/32, steeper above". That is a property of the bilinear transform and it depends on the order, which the row does not say: a roll-off maps to any whole number of orders up to 512, and from order 24 on the bound fails at f_s/32 for every design, whatever the implementation.

In the octave that ends at f, the bilinear transform makes the skirt of an order-n design steeper than n·6.0206 dB by

    n · 20·log₁₀( tan(π f / f_s) / (2·tan(π f / (2 f_s))) ) dB,   about 2.17 · n · (π f / f_s)² dB.

At f = f_s/32 that is 0.0209 dB per order: 0.48 dB at order 23 and 0.50 dB at order 24.

**Proposed wording of the row:** "The slope well below Nyquist. The bilinear transform puts n zeros at Nyquist, so the skirt steepens as it approaches it: in the octave that ends at f it is steeper than n·6.0206 dB by about 2.17·n·(π f/f_s)² dB. It is within ±0.5 dB per octave in octaves that end at or below f_s/32 for an order up to 23, and in octaves that end at or below f_s/(7·√n) for a higher order; steeper above."

The acceptance of WP-419 ("slope within ±0.5 dB/oct where the table says exact or bounded") stays as it is and is tested over the orders the row now covers, 1 to 512.

Other readings, the owner's to choose instead: an order limit (the bound is stated for n ≤ 23 only and nothing is promised above); or a bound that grows with the order (±max(0.5, 0.021·n) dB per octave at f_s/32).

## Why

No implementation can meet the row as written at every order it admits; the package's documentation (`IIRDesign.swift:89–92`) repeats the bound without an order limit, and its test covers orders 2, 4, 8 and 16 only.

## Evidence

`Docs/Process/reports/WP-419-A.md` (A-1), reproduced by the auditor (`audit-m4a-A.md`): octave 750 → 1 500 Hz at 48 kHz, cut-off 10 Hz, through the public API — Butterworth of order 24, 32 and 64: +0.504, +0.672 and +1.343 dB per octave; Chebyshev I (1 dB): +0.511, +0.681, +1.362; the controls of order 16 and 23 pass (+0.336, +0.483). On the verifier's grid 25 of 684 cases are outside, all at orders 24 and 32: Butterworth, Chebyshev I and the three Bessel normalisations, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, low-pass and upper band-pass skirt. The formula above is the integrator's (the ratio of the pre-warped frequencies at the two ends of the octave); it gives 0.4814 dB at order 23 and 0.5023 dB at order 24, against the 0.483 and 0.504 measured. The constant 7 leaves room for the distance of a Chebyshev skirt from its asymptote (0.007 dB at order 24 in the verifier's figures); the fix cycle's test confirms it over the families and orders, or corrects it in this change request.

## Sections affected

§6.3.4 (the table), WP-419, the documentation of `IIRDesign`'s `rollOff:` initialisers and of `BandBoost` with an order.
