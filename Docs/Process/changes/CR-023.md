## What

1. **The crossovers of WP-611c and WP-611d are measured and recorded, not committed as shipped values.** `CrossoverTable.shipped` still holds the initial values of §7.4. Measured in Release on the iOS 27.0 simulator of a loaded host (two runs, the same crossovers): one-shot convolution of 16 384 samples, the transform leads the direct method from 1 024 taps, which is a cost-model factor of 49 where the table ships 4; the mel projection, banded leads dense at every size of the grid, where the table ships 2²⁰; the median, the GPU leads the CPU lanes from 4.5 million values (17 × 513 × 512), where the table ships 2²⁴. With the shipped factor of 4, `.automatic` takes the transform from 49 taps on 4 096 samples, where it is several times slower on this host: speed only, not correctness. The values a release ships are those of a reference device (WP-903a), so the two packages stay `Implementing`.
2. **Wanted from `ZTronDSPCore` and `ZTronDSPMetal` for item 1**: a way to replace the convolution factor of a `CrossoverTable` (the analysis target rebuilds the table through its public initialiser, and a test fails if the table gains a field), and a case of `CrossoverEntry` for the convolution (its measurement is a value of its own, `ConvolutionCrossover`).
3. **References (STG-2).** librosa is not installed: the golden vectors of the onset strength, the tempograms and the separation masks are written from the published definitions with NumPy and SciPy, and each generator says so. Convolution and correlation use `numpy.convolve` and `scipy.signal.correlate` as the table of §12 asks.
4. **Latency of the centred tempogram** is W − ⌊W/2⌋ − 1 frames (191 for the default window of 384), not "W/2" as §6.9 puts it: that is when the last value of the window, centred as the reference centres it, has arrived.
5. **Latency of `HPSS`** is frame − hop + look-ahead · hop samples, not "one frame plus the look-ahead" (§6.10): the delay of this package's `STFT` → `InverseSTFT` pair (CR-019 item 3) plus the look-ahead in samples.
6. **How `HPSS` frames.** With a causal median the recipe frames centred; with a centred median it frames causally, behind lead-in frames of silence that the separation step writes on a complex spectrum. This keeps the stream index aligned for the latency-matched bypass (ACT-8), and it keeps a masked first frame from being divided by a window's edge: framed causally whatever the median, a component of white noise comes back 489 times the signal's peak; as written, at most 1.02 times.
7. **`HarmonicPercussive` switched off is bypassed, not suspended** (it keeps the domain). A bound switch on a *centred* step outside the recipe is not latency-matched, because the core's switched regions delay samples only; the step's documentation says so.
8. **`ConvolutionStrategy`** is one enum in `ZTronDSPDesign`, shared by the `Convolve` stage and the one-shot functions. On a stream `.fft` is the partitioned kernel; one-shot `.partitioned` runs the C kernel block by block; `.automatic` goes partitioned past 2²² transform points; the direct method computes only the values a mode keeps.
9. **`Autocorrelation` may begin a block**, alone or after sequence stages, and is then taken to start from samples; on a novelty curve or a scalar track it is written after the step or fluently. Defaults: `normalization: .none`, `maxLag: nil` (the window less one).
10. **Tempograms.** Rows ascend in tempo and only the rows inside `range:` are emitted; a centred tempogram is led in by a linear ramp; a causal one emits once its window is full. `TempogramMethod` / `TempogramTransform`, `MedianStrategy` / `MedianFilter` and `MaskStrategy` are protocols, so a conformer written outside the package works.
11. **`.unbiased` by a transform method is held to the sums, not to the quotients**: on one fixture (1 024 against 1 024) the quotient at the longest lags is at 1.07·10⁻⁵ of the largest value, against 10⁻⁵, because the division by the few products of the last lags magnifies the transform's round-off.
12. **Masks where neither median reaches the floor are both 0**, as §6.10 says (the reference implementation shares such a bin).
13. **ORD-4 in two places speaks in the compiler's words, not the package's.** `Pipeline { Gain(…); Tempogram() }` on its own is a valid `Pipeline<Novelty, TempoSpectrum>` by the core's rule for a self-typed stage after sequence stages, so the unavailable overload that carried a sentence for it was never chosen by the compiler and was removed; the mistake is refused where the pipeline is attached or follows samples, by the type checker. Likewise for a pipeline that begins with `OnsetStrength` and is attached. Fixtures hold both refusals.
14. **Register row 10 (§7.5)** says the floor of the transform method is relative to the largest output. It is relative to the largest output the inputs *can* give, max|x| · Σ|h|: measured 6.6·10⁻⁷ of that at worst, and 2.0·10⁻⁶ of the largest output there is for the kernel (1, −2, 3, −2, 1) on a full-scale sine. The documentation of `DSP.convolve`, `DSP.correlate`, `ConvolutionStrategy` and `Autocorrelation` states it so. The golden acceptance (10⁻⁵ of the largest output) is met with room.
15. **WP-541 is run on the engine's own threads**, through the public API: a `ThreadAllocationAudit` opened and closed inside a `FeatureCallback` at the head of the stock pipeline of §10.4, for 10 000 blocks on the thread of a source read as fast as possible, and for 300 blocks on the analysis thread with a source paced as a microphone.
16. **Additions to the public surface** the specification does not name: `ConvolutionStrategy`, `ConvolutionMode`, `CorrelationNormalization`, `DSP.correlationFloor`, `TempogramMethod`, `TempogramTransform`, `AutocorrelationTempogram`, `FourierTempogram`, `MedianAxis`, `MedianFilter`, `MedianStrategy` with `SelectionNetworkMedian`, `MetalMedian`, `AutomaticMedian`, `HarmonicPercussiveComponent`, `MaskStrategy` with `SoftMask`, `HardMask`, `DSP.harmonicPercussiveMasks`, `DSP.harmonicPercussive`, `ConvolutionCrossover`, `AnalysisCalibrationReport`, `Calibration.measureAnalysis`, `Calibration.analysisReport`, and `MedianOracle` in `ZTronDSPTesting`.

## Why

Items 1 and 2 are what a simulator on a shared host can and cannot settle. Item 3 is what the host allows. Items 4, 5 and 14 correct figures or wording of the specification by measurement. Item 13 is a limit of what an unavailable overload can catch. The rest are choices the specification leaves to the implementation.
