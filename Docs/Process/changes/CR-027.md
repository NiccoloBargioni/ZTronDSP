## What

The Examples project (`Examples/ZTronDSPGallery`, WP-810) hosts the views in an application; its hosted suites and UI tests ran on the simulators of iOS 17.5, 18.6 and 27.0. The two spikes were to confirm, or correct by change request, the assumptions that WP-819 and WP-823 were built on (CR-021 item 13). They correct them as follows.

### Pinch (PINCH-8)

1. **Row "iOS 18 and later"** (`SpatialEventGesture`): confirmed.
2. **Rows "iOS 17" and "iOS 15 and 16"**: when two fingers land together, the pinch half of the pair reports from the first callback to the last (the centroid, and the ratio of the finger distances), and the simultaneous `DragGesture` reports nothing at any time. The first finger's start location, which the table takes the finger line from, is then not to be had; as built, a pinch with both axes enabled therefore scaled nothing on iOS 17. What the drag reports when the first finger lands before the second could not be produced by a test and stays an assumption.
3. **Proposed wording.** iOS 17 row: "From the pinch's start location (the centroid) to the first finger's start location, which the drag reports only when the first finger lands before the second. When both land together the drag reports nothing for the whole pinch, and the finger line is not known." iOS 15 and 16 row: add "When both fingers land together the drag reports nothing, and the finger line is not known." New sentence under PINCH-3: **"A pinch whose finger line is not known scales every enabled axis."** The other reading — such a pinch scales nothing, and an application that enables both axes has no pinch before iOS 18 unless the first finger lands first — is the owner's to choose instead.
   **Built** on this reading: `PinchTracker` scales every enabled axis, from the first report, when the drag of the iOS 17 or the iOS 15 and 16 path is silent (both fingers landed together); its documentation and that of `.pinchScaling(_:)` say so (PINCH-8).

### Size transitions (§8.9, §8.3, §8.4)

4. **The detection of SZ-4 stands**, each signal for one kind of transition: a change of size that SwiftUI makes, animated or not, moves the view's own bounds once per display frame, the layer carries no animation, and the `Animatable` probe is handed every size; a rotation gives the view its final bounds at once and animates the layer, and the probe is handed the final size once.
5. **What was built on it did not hold.** Three defects of WP-823 as merged:
   - **A frame of a transition was made a step too early.** The surface stepped on its display-link tick; SwiftUI moves the bounds *after* the tick of the same frame, so every frame of a SwiftUI-animated change was committed one animation step behind (18 px in the linear test animation, 31 px with ease-in-out, 65 to 69 px with a spring), on all three systems. The acceptance of §8.9 as worded did not see it, because on the surface's own tick the sizes agree.
   - **The display scale was read from the layer's `contentsScale`**, which `MTKView` rewrites whenever its drawable is sized by hand. In a rotation the drawable shrank to 1 × 1 px and on the way back grew past the largest texture (4 651 × 11 469 px seen): Metal then ends the application. It did so on iOS 18.6 in the hosted suite and on iOS 17.5 in the gallery itself, under a turn of the device there and back, in three runs of six. **An application met this crash by rotating a device.**
   - **The frame-rate cap passed a tick of a transition by** when the display link's timestamps came closer together than a frame (seen on the simulators after a late tick): one frame of a transition was then not made.
   - With the first of these, **a resize without animation cost two draws**, the first at the old size and shown stretched for one frame (iOS 18.6 and 27.0), where §8.4 asks for one.
6. **Proposed wording**, as built (revised on 2026-11-21; where the wording filed on 2026-11-17 differs, each point says how, and why the built rule departs from it).
   - SZ-2, the sentence "on every display frame the drawable size is set to the presentation bounds times the display scale, and one draw is made" becomes: "A frame is due whenever the layer is shown at another size, in whole pixels, than the one the last frame was made for: while Core Animation animates the layer, once per display frame; while SwiftUI moves the view's bounds, in the run-loop turn in which they move. The size is read and the frame is made at the end of that run-loop turn, when SwiftUI and UIKit have laid the view out and before the transaction is committed, not on the display-link tick: SwiftUI moves a view's bounds after the tick of the same frame, and a frame made on the tick is shown one step late. The drawable is given the presentation bounds times the display scale, rounded, and never a size beyond the largest texture of the device. The display scale is the one SwiftUI reports (`\.displayScale`), never the layer's `contentsScale`, which `MTKView` rewrites whenever its drawable is given a size by hand. During a transition nothing is drawn on the tick: what is due for another cause is drawn with the frame at the end of the turn. Before the first drawable, the tick draws." The wording filed on 2026-11-17 added its sentences to SZ-2 and kept "on every display frame … one draw is made"; as built, a display frame in which the size did not change in whole pixels draws nothing, and the size is cut to the largest texture (asking for a drawable beyond it was the crash of item 5). Lint part 1 refuses the name `contentsScale` in the code of `ZTronDSPUI` (SZ-2) since 2026-11-21.
   - SZ-4: "Detecting a transition. Two signals, either of which starts it, each standing for one kind of transition. A change of size that SwiftUI makes, animated or not, moves the view's own bounds, once per display frame while it is animated: the layer carries no animation and is shown at its own size throughout, and an `Animatable` probe in the SwiftUI wrapper is handed every size, in the turn in which the bounds move or, for a change without animation on iOS 18 and later, a frame before them. A rotation of the interface gives the view its final bounds at once and animates the layer itself (its presentation bounds differ from its model bounds, or it has animation keys); the probe is handed the final size once. The probe's report, and the view's announcement of another drawable size or another display scale, are signs that ask for a look at the layer at the end of the turn: the frame is made from what the layer says then, and a report that comes before the bounds move draws nothing. The transition ends at the end of the first display frame in which the layer rests at the size of the last frame and no sign arrived; nothing is drawn then." As filed, the transition ended "when the presentation size equals the model size and neither signal was active in the frame", with "a frame at the exact size" after it when the last one was drawn at another size; as built, the last frame of a transition is already at the size the layer rests at, so nothing is left to draw when it ends.
   - §8.3, cause "drawable size changed", add: "A frame that is due to a change of size is made at the end of the run-loop turn in which the size changed (SZ-2), not on the next tick. A frame of a size transition is not held against the frame-rate cap: for the duration of a transition the surface asks for every tick of the display, up to 120 a second, whatever `.maximumFrameRate` says." The last clause is new: a cap that lets a tick through only so often still drops a frame of a transition when the display ticks faster than the cap. **The owner is asked to confirm it**: it lifts the cap of `.maximumFrameRate` while a transition runs, and no display faster than 60 Hz was at hand to see it there.
   - §8.9, acceptance: "… at every display tick of the transition, **when the frame is made at the end of its run-loop turn, and, for a change of size that SwiftUI makes, also when the frame's transaction is committed**, the drawable's size equals the presentation size within one pixel …". As filed, "when the frame's transaction is committed" for every transition. That holds for every change SwiftUI makes (0.00 to 0.50 px on the three runtimes) and for a rotation on 27.0. For a layer Core Animation animates, the presentation size is a function of the moment it is asked for, on iOS 17.5 in most frames and on 18.6 after a late frame, and it moves on between the draw and the commit (up to 27 px in the runs of 2026-11-18, 105 px after a hold-up on 18.6): measured at the commit, a rotation there fails the acceptance with no frame made late.
   - §8.4, row "View resized without animation: 1 draw per changed size" stays.
   **Built** in the package (Implementer M8b-2, 2026-11-17 and 18: `Scheduling/TurnEnd.swift`, a run-loop observer ahead of Core Animation's commit; `SurfaceCoordinator`, `MetalSurface`, `SurfaceHost`, `FrameScheduler`), the rule met every cell of the acceptance as proposed here in every run on the three systems; the figures of the integrated tree are under Evidence.

### A component in a UIKit host

7. **A view hosted in a `UIHostingController` reads `\.scenePhase` as `.background` for its whole life**, in an application too (CR-021 item 11 had seen it in the test process). The components present only while the phase is `.active` (§8.3), so such a component makes its Metal view and never draws. A SwiftUI `App` scene, a navigation destination and a sheet are told `.active` and draw. Under the UIKit boundary there is nothing else the package may read. **Proposed (the reading that needs no exception to §8.6):** the components keep reading `\.scenePhase`; their documentation says that a host which is not a SwiftUI scene hands the phase of its scene down with `.environment(\.scenePhase, …)`; a surface made under `.background` that has seen no other phase by the time its feed is live says so once in the log. The alternatives are the owner's: let the bridge read the activation state of the view's window scene (an exception to UB-3 and UB-4), or treat "background since the view appeared" as "not supplied" (not recommended: a view made while the application really is in the background would draw).
   **Built** on the proposed reading: the notice is an `os.Logger` message (subsystem `com.zombietron.ztrondsp`, category `surface`, level notice), said once per surface, when its feed is live and it has read no phase but `.background` since it was made; the documentation of the heat-map views, of `WaveformView` and of `SurfaceHost` says how a host that is not a SwiftUI scene hands the phase down.

### The Examples project

8. **The gallery's tests reach the package's probes with `@testable import`** (the render, surface, axis and pipeline-host probes and the clock source are internal environment keys). The application itself imports only Foundation, SwiftUI and the two products. If the owner wants the gallery's tests free of `@testable`, the probes and the clock source move to `ZTronDSPTesting`. Since 2026-11-21 lint part 3 (EXT-1) refuses `@testable import` in the application (`Examples/ZTronDSPGallery/Gallery/`); its hosted tests keep it until the owner decides.
9. **The gallery's test targets import UIKit for more than hosting** (UB-6): to host a SwiftUI view in the application, to find the Metal view and its layer, to flip the switch behind a `Toggle`, and to ask the scene for an interface orientation. Proposed: UB-6 says so for these targets. Since 2026-11-21 lint part 1 holds the application to UB-1 and UB-2 without the bridge's exception; the test targets stay outside part 1 until UB-6 says what they may import.
10. **Gates.** Proposed: gate 1 builds the gallery's application (Release); gate 5 runs its hosted suites on each runtime of the matrix; its UI tests (the spikes and a walk through every screen, about a quarter of an hour) are not a gate. The gallery stays outside the coverage denominator (§4.2). **Built** on 2026-11-21 (`Scripts/ci.sh`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`): gate 1 builds the application in Release, in derived data of its own; gate 5 runs the hosted suites on each simulator of the run and fails when they record a known issue (none may stand there now); the UI tests are the nightly job `gallery-ui-nightly` (`Scripts/ci.sh --nightly`, and on a dispatch that asks for them), not a gate. §10.3 says none of this yet.

## Why

Items 1 to 6 are what the rows of WP-818 and WP-822 ask for. Item 5 corrects merged packages before their verification; its second defect ends an application. Item 7 is a gap between §8.3 and §8.6 that only the owner can close otherwise.
