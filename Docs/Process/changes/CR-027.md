## What

The Examples project (`Examples/ZTronDSPGallery`, WP-810) hosts the views in an application; its hosted suites and UI tests ran on the simulators of iOS 17.5, 18.6 and 27.0. The two spikes were to confirm, or correct by change request, the assumptions that WP-819 and WP-823 were built on (CR-021 item 13). They correct them as follows.

### Pinch (PINCH-8)

1. **Row "iOS 18 and later"** (`SpatialEventGesture`): confirmed.
2. **Rows "iOS 17" and "iOS 15 and 16"**: when two fingers land together, the pinch half of the pair reports from the first callback to the last (the centroid, and the ratio of the finger distances), and the simultaneous `DragGesture` reports nothing at any time. The first finger's start location, which the table takes the finger line from, is then not to be had; as built, a pinch with both axes enabled therefore scaled nothing on iOS 17. What the drag reports when the first finger lands before the second could not be produced by a test and stays an assumption.
3. **Proposed wording.** iOS 17 row: "From the pinch's start location (the centroid) to the first finger's start location, which the drag reports only when the first finger lands before the second. When both land together the drag reports nothing for the whole pinch, and the finger line is not known." iOS 15 and 16 row: add "When both fingers land together the drag reports nothing, and the finger line is not known." New sentence under PINCH-3: **"A pinch whose finger line is not known scales every enabled axis."** The other reading — such a pinch scales nothing, and an application that enables both axes has no pinch before iOS 18 unless the first finger lands first — is the owner's to choose instead.

### Size transitions (§8.9, §8.3, §8.4)

4. **The detection of SZ-4 stands**, each signal for one kind of transition: a change of size that SwiftUI makes, animated or not, moves the view's own bounds once per display frame, the layer carries no animation, and the `Animatable` probe is handed every size; a rotation gives the view its final bounds at once and animates the layer, and the probe is handed the final size once.
5. **What was built on it did not hold.** Three defects of WP-823 as merged:
   - **A frame of a transition was made a step too early.** The surface stepped on its display-link tick; SwiftUI moves the bounds *after* the tick of the same frame, so every frame of a SwiftUI-animated change was committed one animation step behind (18 px in the linear test animation, 31 px with ease-in-out, 65 to 69 px with a spring), on all three systems. The acceptance of §8.9 as worded did not see it, because on the surface's own tick the sizes agree.
   - **The display scale was read from the layer's `contentsScale`**, which `MTKView` rewrites whenever its drawable is sized by hand. In a rotation the drawable shrank to 1 × 1 px and on the way back grew past the largest texture (4 651 × 11 469 px seen): Metal then ends the application. It did so on iOS 18.6 in the hosted suite and on iOS 17.5 in the gallery itself, under a turn of the device there and back, in three runs of six. **An application met this crash by rotating a device.**
   - **The frame-rate cap passed a tick of a transition by** when the display link's timestamps came closer together than a frame (seen on the simulators after a late tick): one frame of a transition was then not made.
   - With the first of these, **a resize without animation cost two draws**, the first at the old size and shown stretched for one frame (iOS 18.6 and 27.0), where §8.4 asks for one.
6. **Proposed wording.**
   - SZ-2, add: "The size is read and the frame is made at the end of the run-loop turn of that frame, when SwiftUI and UIKit have laid the view out and before the transaction is committed, not on the display-link tick: SwiftUI moves a view's bounds after the tick of the same frame, and a frame made on the tick is shown one step late. The display scale is the one SwiftUI reports (`\.displayScale`), never the layer's `contentsScale`, which `MTKView` rewrites whenever its drawable is given a size by hand."
   - SZ-4: "Detecting a transition. Two signals, either of which starts it, each standing for one kind of transition. A change of size that SwiftUI makes, animated or not, moves the view's own bounds, once per display frame while it is animated: the layer carries no animation and is shown at its own size throughout, and an `Animatable` probe in the SwiftUI wrapper is handed every size, in the turn in which the bounds move or, for a change without animation on iOS 18 and later, a frame before them. A rotation of the interface gives the view its final bounds at once and animates the layer itself (its presentation bounds differ from its model bounds, or it has animation keys); the probe is handed the final size once. The transition ends when the presentation size equals the model size and neither signal was active in the frame; a frame at the exact size follows only if the last one was drawn at another size."
   - §8.3, cause "drawable size changed", add: "A frame that is due to a change of size is made at the end of the run-loop turn in which the size changed (SZ-2), not on the next tick. A frame of a size transition is not held against the frame-rate cap."
   - §8.9, acceptance: "… at every display tick of the transition, **when the frame's transaction is committed**, the drawable's size equals the presentation size within one pixel …".
   - §8.4, row "View resized without animation: 1 draw per changed size" stays.
   A prototype of this rule in the gallery's test target met every cell of the acceptance in every run on the three systems (0.00 to 0.50 px at commit, the midpoint frame identical to the direct rendering, draws equal to frames, nothing uploaded, the exact size at both ends of a rotation).

### A component in a UIKit host

7. **A view hosted in a `UIHostingController` reads `\.scenePhase` as `.background` for its whole life**, in an application too (CR-021 item 11 had seen it in the test process). The components present only while the phase is `.active` (§8.3), so such a component makes its Metal view and never draws. A SwiftUI `App` scene, a navigation destination and a sheet are told `.active` and draw. Under the UIKit boundary there is nothing else the package may read. **Proposed (the reading that needs no exception to §8.6):** the components keep reading `\.scenePhase`; their documentation says that a host which is not a SwiftUI scene hands the phase of its scene down with `.environment(\.scenePhase, …)`; a surface made under `.background` that has seen no other phase by the time its feed is live says so once in the log. The alternatives are the owner's: let the bridge read the activation state of the view's window scene (an exception to UB-3 and UB-4), or treat "background since the view appeared" as "not supplied" (not recommended: a view made while the application really is in the background would draw).

### The Examples project

8. **The gallery's tests reach the package's probes with `@testable import`** (the render, surface, axis and pipeline-host probes and the clock source are internal environment keys). The application itself imports only Foundation, SwiftUI and the two products. If the owner wants the gallery's tests free of `@testable`, the probes and the clock source move to `ZTronDSPTesting`.
9. **The gallery's test targets import UIKit for more than hosting** (UB-6): to host a SwiftUI view in the application, to find the Metal view and its layer, to flip the switch behind a `Toggle`, and to ask the scene for an interface orientation. Proposed: UB-6 says so for these targets.
10. **Gates.** Proposed: gate 1 builds the gallery's application (Release); gate 5 runs its hosted suites on each runtime of the matrix; its UI tests (the spikes and a walk through every screen, about a quarter of an hour) are not a gate. The gallery stays outside the coverage denominator (§4.2).

## Why

Items 1 to 6 are what the rows of WP-818 and WP-822 ask for. Item 5 corrects merged packages before their verification; its second defect ends an application. Item 7 is a gap between §8.3 and §8.6 that only the owner can close otherwise.

## Evidence

`Examples/ZTronDSPGallery/Spikes/WP-818-findings.md` and `WP-822-findings.md`, with the raw logs under `Spikes/logs/ios-17.5`, `ios-18.6` and `ios-27.0` (the recorder's journals per display frame, the verdicts of every acceptance run, the crash texts). Hosted suites: 23 tests in 8 suites, three runs in a row on iOS 27.0, and on 18.6 and 17.5. WP-811 (R-25): a `Toggle` over `@State` bypasses and restores a stage within the run-loop turn of the write and one block, and 200 blocks with the session running evaluate the owning view's body not once. §8.4 through real hosting: eight scenarios of nine as written on all three systems, and the real background-and-return cycle; the ninth is the two-draw resize of item 5.

## Not done, and why

- The corrections of items 3, 6 and 7 in the package: under way by the same implementer, who then removes the known-issue marks from the hosted suites.
- A device: what the display shows, the display link's timing there, faster displays. iOS 15 and 16 (their runtimes do not run on this host). A second finger that lands after the first, and free two-finger paths: no public API synthesises them.
